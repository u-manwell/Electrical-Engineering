# Electrical-Engineering
Compilations of problems found in Circuit Analysis I &amp; II coursework, and more...

As of December 2025, this is currently a small scale project inspired by what I would have liked to have during my first and second-year studies at Columbia University as an EE major.
My area of interest consists of analog devices and circuits, which is mostly the content which I will cover. However, I look forward to having a mix of Signal & System, E&M, ODEs, and Linear Algebra content on the way. As I am just an undergrad and refuse to (accidently) spread misinformation, I will restrict the content to purely problems and solutions. However, I do intend to push out theory in the future only after intensively proofreading my drafts.
If any discrepency is found in my solutions (or would like to make suggestions to the material) please email mpg2169@columbia.edu with the subject line [Github_EE].

## File Structure
Found in PROBLEMS_FILES are subfolders with specific topic sections and respective problems and solutions. Not all problems have detailed solutions. Instead, one problem will have detailed solutions to guide readers on how to approach similiar types of problems, while others will just have qualitative/quantitative answers. Seeing circuit problems as matrices (although they are) or a problem solving process is not at all engineering, and seeing it as such is a waste of time for your own good. It is necessary to understand circuits mathematically, but being able to reason voltage drops, resonance, or gain intuitively is orders of magnitude more important and beneficial as an electrical engineer.

Found in the SIM_FILES folder are pre-drawn LTspice schematics for respective problems, in respective sections. Schematics have pre-selected simulation instructions (operating point, transient, and ac sims), so all users have to do is run the simulations (and click to view respective nodes if running transient sims).
